I think about AI like my porch light. Every night I decide when to flip it on. In winter I turn it on early because the sun drops by five. In summer I wait until almost nine. The decision shifts with the season, the weather, how dark the sky looks that night. There's no fixed rule. It's judgment against changing conditions, and that's exactly the kind of call AI is good at. Fuzzy inputs, shifting context, a "close enough" answer that adapts to what's in front of it.
What I don't do is use AI to run the circuit behind that light. I don't ask it to trip my breaker, pull power off the grid, and meter the right load into a 60-watt bulb. That path is deterministic. The voltage is the voltage, the wiring is the wiring, and the answer has to be exact every single time or something breaks or burns. You don't want a probabilistic system anywhere near work that demands repeatable, verifiable output. You want code that does the same thing on the thousandth run as it did on the first.
So that's the line. Use AI for the judgment calls where context shifts and good enough beats rigid. Keep it out of the deterministic path where the only acceptable answer is the correct one, every time. Knowing which side of that line you're on is most of the job.
